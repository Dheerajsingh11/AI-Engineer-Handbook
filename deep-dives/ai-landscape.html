11.3

The AI Landscape & Ecosystem

A field map of who and what is in AI today. This page surveys the players (labs, cloud providers, open community), the model families, the layers of the stack from silicon to application, and the open questions — so you can read AI news, place any new tool, and understand where value and risk sit in the ecosystem.

Section 01 BeginnerStart from zero

Assumed knowledge

The AI/ML/GenAI map (Topic 11.1). This page is the who's-who and what's-what that fills that map in.

The AI world can feel like a wall of names — labs, models, tools, frameworks — announced weekly. This page gives you a stable map so new names slot into place instead of adding to the noise. The key insight: AI is a stack of layers, and every company and tool lives at one of them.

The AI stack, bottom to top
LayerWhat it isWho plays here
HardwareChips that run AI (GPUs, TPUs)NVIDIA, Google (TPU), AMD, cloud silicon
Cloud & computeWhere models are trained and servedAWS, Azure, Google Cloud (Topic 10.1)
Foundation modelsThe large models everything builds onOpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Mistral, DeepSeek
Model access & toolingAPIs, frameworks, orchestrationThe model APIs, LangChain, LlamaIndex, Hugging Face (Phases 7, 9)
ApplicationsProducts people actually useEveryone — including what you will build
Where you sit

Most AI engineering jobs — including the one this handbook prepares you for — live in the top two layers: building applications on top of foundation models, using the tooling layer. You do not build the chips or train the frontier models; you compose them into products. That is not a lesser role — it is where most of the value reaches users, and it is the layer with the most demand. Knowing the layers below you helps you make good choices; you rarely work in them.

A defining tension: open vs closed. Much of the field's dynamics comes from the split between closed frontier models (accessed by API, most capable, controlled) and open-weight models (downloadable, customisable, catching up). It shapes cost, privacy, control and strategy (Topics 9.1, 9.2). You do not have to pick a side — good systems often use both — but understanding the tension explains a lot of the news and a lot of enterprise decisions.

Section 03 IntermediateBeyond text: the modalities

Language models are the centre of attention, but generative AI spans modalities, each a live area with its own models and tools.

Generative AI by modality
ModalityDoesCovered in
TextChat, writing, code, reasoningPhases 1, 3–9 — the handbook's core
Images (understand)Read images, documents, chartsMultimodal AI (Topic 6.2)
Images (generate)Create images from textDiffusion (Topic 12.5), Generative Media
Audio / speechTranscribe, synthesise, converseSpeech & Audio AI
VideoGenerate and understand videoGenerative Media
MultimodalCombine several at onceTopic 6.2

The trend is convergence. Increasingly, one model handles several modalities — read an image, discuss it, generate a chart. For an application builder this simplifies things: you reason about "a model" that happens to accept text and images, rather than stitching separate systems together. But the specialised generative models (image, audio, video) remain distinct tools with their own ecosystems, covered in their own topics.

Section 04 AdvancedWhere value and risk actually sit

For someone building a career or a product, it helps to see where durable value accrues in this stack — and where the moats are thin.

Value dynamics by layer
LayerMoatNote for you
HardwareVery high (capital, expertise)Concentrated; a strategic chokepoint
Foundation modelsHigh but erodingCapabilities converge; prices fall; commoditising
Tooling / infraMediumFast-moving; today's leader may not be tomorrow's
ApplicationsBuilt on domain, data, distributionWhere a non-lab builds durable value
The implication for what you build

Because foundation models are commoditising (Topic 9.1), a thin wrapper around a model API has no moat — anyone can build it, and the model provider might build it themselves. Durable application value comes from things the model does not give you for free: proprietary data, deep domain integration, workflow and distribution, and reliability. That is exactly what your RAG, agent, evaluation and deployment skills (Phases 4–10) produce. The engineering in this handbook is what turns "we call an LLM" into a product with a defensible edge.

Section 05 ProfessionalThe open questions

To read the field like a professional, know the debates that are genuinely unsettled — so you are neither over-sold nor over-skeptical.

Live, unresolved questions
QuestionWhy it is open
Does scaling keep working?Returns may be diminishing; data may run short (Topic 11.2)
How capable will agents get?Reliability is still the bottleneck (Phase 08)
Open vs closed — who wins?Open models are closing the gap; unclear how far
Can hallucination be solved?It may be intrinsic to the approach (Topic 1.3)
What are the real economics?Training and inference costs vs revenue still shaking out
Safety & regulationGovernance is being written now (Topic 6.4)
Calibrate, don't pick a tribe

The discourse splits into hype ("AGI imminent, everything changes") and dismissal ("just autocomplete, a bubble"). Both are miscalibrated. The grounded position: today's systems are genuinely useful and genuinely limited — transformative for some tasks, unreliable for others, improving fast but not magic. Your value as an engineer is being the person in the room who can tell which is which for a specific problem, because you understand the mechanics (Topic 1.3) and have measured the reality (Topic 4.7). That calibration is worth more than any prediction.

Section 06 ProfessionalExercises

Do these — roughly an hour
  1. Take ten AI company/tool names from recent news and place each on the Section 01 stack.
  2. For a product idea you have, identify what its moat would be beyond "we call an LLM" (Section 04).
  3. Pick one open question from Section 05 and write the strongest one-paragraph case for each side.
  4. Follow one high-signal AI news source for a week (see resources). Notice how much is genuinely new vs repackaged.
  5. Map three modalities to a product that uses each. Which could a single multimodal model now replace?
